A teachers' guide to Sharpwise

How Sharpwise works on shared classroom devices, why it needs no accounts and keeps no pupil data, and how its topics line up with the curriculum.

Words in quotation marks, such as For grown-ups, appear exactly like that in the game.

Shared classroom devices

Sharpwise plays in the web browser at sharpwise.co.uk/play, on a tablet, a laptop or the computer behind a whiteboard. There is nothing to install and nobody signs in. Once the game has loaded, it keeps working without an internet connection.

A ninja for each child, up to four on a device

Each child plays as their own ninja, with their own progress, coins and certificates. One device holds up to four ninjas. A child adds themselves: at the bottom of the Sky Map, they tap Who is playing?, then the New ninja card. Once a device has two or more ninjas, the game opens on Who is playing?, so each child picks their own.

Guest play, for a device many children share

Four is the most one device can hold. When more children than that take turns on a device, use guest play. In the grown-ups' area, Play as a guest lets a child play without a ninja. Nothing a guest does is saved, and the ninjas already on the device are left alone. To finish, open the grown-ups' area again and tap End guest play.

The grown-ups' area

At the bottom of the Sky Map, tap For grown-ups. The game asks a multiplication question written in words, such as thirty-seven times twenty-four. Type the answer and tap Enter. It is there to keep younger children out of the settings; it is not a password.

Playing as a whole class

Whiteboard mode, next to Play as a guest in the grown-ups' area, is made for a class sharing one big screen. The bubbles fly more slowly, questions are read aloud, and tapping a topic opens a relaxed round of ten questions with no lives and no clock. Like guest play, it saves nothing.

While Whiteboard mode is on, the islands also offer Daily Ten: ten maths questions from that year, from easy to hard, as a quick starter. Daily Ten · 60 seconds is the same against the clock.

For two players or two teams on one device, choose Ninja Duel on any island. Before picking the topic, tap Class teams: Off so that it says On, and the two halves of the screen become Team A and Team B. Both sides get the same question, and the first correct slice wins the round. A match is ten rounds.

No accounts and no pupil data

Sharpwise has no accounts. Nobody signs up, logs in or gives an email address, so there is nothing to set up for a class and no list of pupils to give us.

A ninja's name, progress and settings are kept on the device that the child plays on. The game has no adverts and no analytics, and we never receive anything about the children who play. A first name or a nickname is all a ninja needs.

A grown-up can choose to send some things, each from the grown-ups' area or after its quick grown-ups' question, and each one goes only where they send it: a save code, a progress summary, a certificate, a challenge code, today's Daily Sprint result, or the spelling words still to learn.

Reading aloud uses a voice that the browser or device provides. Some of those voices are online voices run by the company that makes the browser or device, and the words being spoken then go to that company to be turned into speech. With read aloud on, those words can include praise with the ninja's name in it. Read aloud is switched on and off for each ninja with the speaking-face button at the top of the Sky Map.

Because progress lives on the device, clearing the browser's data clears it too. There is no class view and no teacher login: the grown-ups' area on each device shows the progress of the ninja playing on it. The privacy page has the details, including how the Android app can be part of the device's own backup.

What it covers

Sharpwise covers maths and English from Reception to Year 6. Reception topics follow the EYFS Early Learning Goals. Years 1 to 6 follow the National Curriculum for England.

Each school year is an island on the Sky Map, with its topics under Maths and Writing. The curriculum page links a page for each school year, which lists every topic of that year with the part of the curriculum it practises and the parts not covered yet. It is made from the game's own list of topics, so it changes when the game does.

Tables Check practice

The Year 4 island has two ways to practise for the multiplication tables check. Both are practice, with the same timing as the Year 4 check.

Tables Check practice gives three warm-up questions that do not count, then 25 questions from the 2 to 12 times tables, 6 seconds each. The child slices the answer from four bubbles.

Tables Check on the number pad is the same practice, but the child types each answer on a number pad.

Tables Check practice is practice only, and it is not the official multiplication tables check. Its scores are kept on the device with the rest of the ninja's progress. They leave it only if a grown-up shares a progress summary, which includes the best and the latest practice score.

At the end of a practice run, the child sees their score out of 25 and each fact they missed. In the grown-ups' area, Check readiness shows the last few practice scores and the tables to work on.

Some children have extra time for timed questions. Under Settings in the grown-ups' area, Extra time (×1.5) gives every timed question one and a half times as long, so 9 seconds in Tables Check practice. No time limit takes the clock off each question, though a Ninja Sprint gets 90 seconds instead. Both apply to the ninja who is playing. Guest play and Whiteboard mode always use the normal time, and while either is on, the grown-ups' area shows only End guest play.
